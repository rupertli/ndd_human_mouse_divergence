How to interpret these rankings

For each gene and cell class, divergence D = (1 − Spearman rho)/2 + normalized z-RMSE + (1 − direction agreement) + normalized peak distance. Peak distance uses positions in the full phase order, divided by 4 for five-bin cortex or 3 for four-phase cerebellum. Normalized z-RMSE is the raw z-RMSE divided by 2, placing it on a 0–1 scale; the raw value is retained in the data. Higher scores (0–4) indicate greater trajectory divergence, not statistical significance or expression fold change. Website normalization changes composite scores and rankings relative to the raw analysis outputs.

Maximum across cell classes: genes are ranked by their highest eligible cell-class score. Least divergent reverses this same ranking: it selects genes whose worst-case score is lowest, not genes with just one similar cell class. Summary medians and means cover all eligible cell classes. Selected cell class: ranking and summaries use only that cell class.

Cell classes are study-defined groupings of source annotations harmonized for cross-species comparison. They may contain multiple cell types and developmental states, and their composition may differ between species and developmental phases.

Constant trajectories can have undefined correlation and divergence scores. Undefined values are omitted from summary medians/means and never treated as zero divergence. Genes without a finite score in the selected scope are excluded from ranking. Details show the number of scored versus tested cell classes; finite maxima cannot establish similarity in unscored cell classes.

The cerebral dataset represents cortex, not the entire cerebrum. Retained cortical comparisons cover six classes: excitatory, inhibitory, astroglial, oligodendrocyte lineage, microglia, and vascular. Astroglial and oligodendrocyte-lineage trajectories have four shared bins; other classes have five. Cerebellum covers five different populations. Maximum scores are sensitive to unequal cell-class coverage and outliers. Cortical mouse replicates mix Gao donors and Di Bella pools; mouse cerebellar samples are individual mice. Cross-region overlap is descriptive, not a matched-cell-class or matched-development regional test.

Cortical alignment uses the Workman 2013 baseline model: event score = (ln(postconception days) − constant)/slope, with human constants 3.167/3.720 and mouse 2.145/1.894. Mouse birth is assumed at postconception day 18.5. Analyst-selected bin edges are 0.20, 0.35, 0.50, 0.65, 0.80, and 1.00; these are not published biological phases and do not apply cortical-neurogenesis corrections. Out-of-range ages are excluded from comparisons. Known overlapping Wang donors and ambiguously multipotent new populations are excluded. Remaining donor overlap, mixed regions/assays, and symbol-based rather than verified one-to-one orthology limit interpretation. This is not a disease-effect or confirmatory NDD analysis.
