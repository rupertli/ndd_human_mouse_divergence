How to interpret these rankings

For each gene and cell class, divergence D = (1 − Spearman rho)/2 + normalized z-RMSE + (1 − direction agreement) + peak-phase distance/3. Normalized z-RMSE is the raw z-RMSE divided by 2, placing it on a 0–1 scale; the raw value is retained in the data. Higher scores (0–4) indicate greater trajectory divergence, not statistical significance or expression fold change.

Maximum across cell classes: genes are ranked by their highest eligible cell-class score. Least divergent reverses this same ranking: it selects genes whose worst-case score is lowest, not genes with just one similar cell class. Summary medians and means cover all eligible cell classes. Selected cell class: ranking and summaries use only that cell class.

Cell classes are study-defined groupings of source annotations harmonized for cross-species comparison. They may contain multiple cell types and developmental states, and their composition may differ between species and developmental phases.

Constant trajectories can have undefined correlation and divergence scores. Undefined values are omitted from summary medians/means and never treated as zero divergence. Genes without a finite score in the selected scope are excluded from ranking. Details show the number of scored versus tested cell classes; finite maxima cannot establish similarity in unscored cell classes.

The cerebral dataset represents cortex, not the entire cerebrum. Retained cortical comparisons cover two populations; cerebellum covers five different populations. Maximum scores are sensitive to unequal cell-class coverage and outliers. Human donors and pooled mouse cortical libraries differ in replication; mouse cerebellar samples are individual mice. Cross-region overlap is descriptive and is not a matched-cell-class regional test.
